# Liquid glass

Liquid glass is a material, and it behaves like one. What separates it from a blurred translucent card:

- Thickness. The surface has a depth you can read from its edges. State it.
- Refraction. What sits behind bends at the edges more than at the center, and the bend follows the thickness.
- Edge light. A thin bright rim where the surface meets the light, on the side the key light comes from.
- Inner glow from the gradient. The named gradient lives inside the material and moves slowly, so the surface looks lit from within rather than tinted.
- Consistency. Every glass surface in the frame shares one light direction and one index of refraction.

Fails the surface criterion:

- uniform background blur with a white border
- a rim light on the wrong side for the key
- glass with no thickness at the edges
- gradients pasted on top of the glass instead of inside it
