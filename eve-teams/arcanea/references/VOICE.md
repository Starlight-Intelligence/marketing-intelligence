# Voice rules for Arcanea copy

Byline: Claude (Sonnet 5.5), 2026-10-21, unreviewed.

Who reads this: the voice-editor seat and any drafting seat. Use it as a checklist before a draft goes to Frank. A draft that trips any rule in part 1 goes back. Part 2 is a set of proposals for Frank to rule on; none of it is decided.

This file contains no Arcanea canon. The example sentences are invented for illustration and about a made-up generic fantasy setting, so they do not describe Arcanea.

## Part 1. Rules that stop AI-sounding prose

Each rule names the pattern, why readers catch it, then a weak line and a rewrite. The rewrites add a concrete fact, a number or a scene, because most AI prose fails by being replaceable: any brand could have written it.

### 1. The not-X-but-Y contrast
Why it fails: it sets up a claim nobody made so it can knock it down. It is the most common tell in generated copy.
- Weak: "This is not just a map, it is a living world."
- Rewrite: "The map has 41 named rivers. Nine of them change course between chapter two and chapter six."
Rule: state the Y directly. If X was never claimed, delete it.

### 2. The one-line closer
Why it fails: a short punchy sentence at the end of every paragraph reads as a template.
- Weak: "We spent two years on the lore. The result speaks for itself."
- Rewrite: "We spent two years on the lore, and the first reader email asked why the harbor tax is paid in salt." End on the detail.
Rule: end a paragraph on its last piece of information. Never add a sentence whose only job is rhythm.

### 3. The rule of three
Why it fails: three parallel adjectives or clauses is a reflex, usually with one filler item.
- Weak: "Bold, immersive, and unforgettable."
- Rewrite: "Forty minutes of a single unbroken camera move through a flooded library."
Rule: use the number of items the content has. Two is fine. Four is fine.

### 4. Stock words
Banned unless quoting someone else: delve, tapestry, realm (outside a literal realm), testament, embark, unleash, elevate, seamless, vibrant, bustling, nestled, ever-evolving, game-changer, journey (as a metaphor), unlock, leverage, landscape (as a metaphor), "in a world where".
- Weak: "Embark on a journey through a vibrant tapestry of magic."
- Rewrite: "Walk the salt road from the harbor to the glass monastery. It takes three days and you can be robbed twice."
Rule: replace the word with the thing it was gesturing at.

### 5. Em dashes and decorative punctuation
Why it fails: generated text leans on em dashes for fake drama.
- Weak: "The sword was forged in silence — and it remembers."
- Rewrite: "The sword was forged in silence. It remembers every owner's name and gets the order wrong."
Rule: no em dashes. Use a period, a comma or a colon.

### 6. Decorative bold and header-for-everything
Why it fails: bold on every third phrase and a heading over every two lines reads as a slide deck, not a person.
- Weak: a post with five bolded phrases and four sub-headings in 300 words.
- Rewrite: plain paragraphs. One bold phrase at most, and only for something a reader must not miss, like a date.

### 7. The announcement opener
Why it fails: "We are thrilled to announce" tells the reader nothing and nobody talks that way.
- Weak: "We are thrilled to announce the launch of our new character."
- Rewrite: "Meet Ilke, the courier who cannot read. Her first design sheet is up today."
Rule: open with the subject, a person, a number or a scene.

### 8. Empty intensifiers and hedges
Why it fails: "truly", "really", "incredibly", "it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember" add length and no content.
- Weak: "It's worth noting that this is a truly incredible design."
- Rewrite: "The cloak has 14 folds and each one is a different guild's colour."

### 9. Rhetorical question then answer
Why it fails: "What makes it special? Everything." is a tic.
- Weak: "So what sets this world apart? The details."
- Rewrite: name one detail.

### 10. Summary paragraph that repeats the post
Why it fails: "In conclusion" and "Ultimately" restate what the reader just read.
- Weak: "Ultimately, this shows how worldbuilding matters."
- Rewrite: end on the last fact, or on the next thing the reader can do and where.

### 11. Abstract nouns instead of people and objects
Why it fails: "storytelling", "experiences", "immersion", "engagement" stand in for the thing.
- Weak: "Deliver immersive storytelling experiences."
- Rewrite: "Each chapter ships with the in-world letter the narrator would have kept."

### 12. Even sentence length and tidy symmetry
Why it fails: human writing has uneven sentences, fragments, and a few odd specifics.
Rule: read the draft aloud. If three sentences in a row are within five words of each other in length, merge or cut one.

### 13. Superlatives with no evidence
- Weak: "The most epic fantasy universe ever."
- Rewrite: say what was counted. a figure like "88 pages of notes for one trade guild" is fine only if true and checkable.

### The specificity test
Every piece must contain at least one fact, number, name or scene that only this project has. If you can swap in another brand's name and the piece still works, cut it and rewrite from the specific.

### The outlier comparison
Before a draft ships, open two files from `references/outliers/` in the same category and ask: does this draft make the same kind of move, or does it only imitate the surface? The outlier files list what not to copy for that reason.

## Part 2. Arcanea voice hypotheses (all PROPOSED, for Frank to rule on)

These are guesses from what an anime/fantasy creative IP tends to need, not from canon. Frank accepts, edits or rejects each one. Until ruled on, drafting seats treat them as untested and must not present them as established voice.

| # | Hypothesis | Status | Frank's ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | The voice is a field archivist: it reports the world as documents and observations, and does not hype it. | PROPOSED | |
| H2 | First person plural ("we") is for studio process posts only. World posts use no narrator pronoun. | PROPOSED | |
| H3 | Every public post carries one concrete in-world object, place or person, never an abstract promise. | PROPOSED | |
| H4 | Warmth comes from precision and credit to makers, not from exclamation marks. Exclamation marks are rare. | PROPOSED | |
| H5 | Lore is released in small dated drops, with the next drop named, rather than in long walls of text. | PROPOSED | |
| H6 | Character reveals lead with what the character wants or cannot do, then the design. | PROPOSED | |
| H7 | Prices, dates and what the reader gets are stated plainly in launch copy, with no countdown pressure. | PROPOSED | |
| H8 | Humour is dry and small, a throwaway line inside a fact, not a joke set-up. | PROPOSED | |
| H9 | Frank's own posts keep a personal register: first person, process, doubts included. The brand account stays more reserved. | PROPOSED | |
| H10 | The team never describes the work as "epic", "stunning" or "immersive". Readers decide that. | PROPOSED | |

Open questions for Frank: which of H1 to H10 to adopt, whether the brand and Frank's personal account should share one voice, and whether to name a human reference voice from the outlier files as the target register.
