## Evidence it outperformed
- Guinness World Records lists the record for most money pledged for a Kickstarter TV series project at $11,385,449 (https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/569730-most-money-pledged-for-a-kickstarter-tv-series-project, search result only).
- D&D Beyond gives 88,887 backers (https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/475-what-does-critical-roles-record-breaking-11, search result only). Reports say it passed $1 million in under an hour, and was at the time the fifth most-backed and fifth most-funded campaign on the platform (via the search summary; source article not opened).
- Compared against earlier film and TV Kickstarters, which it topped. The campaign later led to a Prime Video series; see https://www.pajiba.com/tv_reviews/the-legend-of-vox-machina-brings-tabletop-roleplaying-to-glorious-life-on-prime.php (search result only).

## The move that transfers
The pitch turns an existing audience's wish into a visible vote: the fans already wanted the show, and the campaign gave them a counter and a way to make it real. Funding goals framed as stages of a finished production (an episode count the money buys) make each dollar legible. Strongest transferable idea: sell a specific, finite thing the community has already asked for, and let the progress bar do the persuading.

## What not to copy
It depended on a large existing live-stream audience; a cold audience will not respond the same way. The tabletop-actors-become-animated-characters angle is specific to this IP.
