## Evidence it outperformed
No traffic figure verified. Strongest public evidence is durable citation: a16z published a named follow-up, "1,000 True Fans? Try 100" (https://a16z.com/1000-true-fans-try-100/, seen in search results), and critics wrote full rebuttals, for example Dave Karpf (https://davekarpf.substack.com/p/the-hollow-core-of-kevin-kellys-thousand) and another Substack titled "1000 true fans is bullsht" (https://shonistar.substack.com/p/1000-true-fans-is-bullsht). Gigazine covered it in 2023 (https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20230107-1000-true-fans). Compared against typical blog essays, which are rarely re-argued 12 or more years later, this is a clear outlier. Exact shares and views: not verified.

## The move that transfers
The essay compresses a diffuse idea about audience size into one memorable number and a back-of-envelope sum (a modest sum per fan times a small crowd equals a living). A concrete figure gives readers something to repeat, adopt, or attack, and all three spread the piece. Any creator can name a specific, checkable threshold for their own field instead of arguing in generalities.

## What not to copy
The 1,000 and $100 figures themselves, and the "true fan" label, are now tied to Kelly and widely disputed. Inventing a similar number without a real derivation reads as imitation.
