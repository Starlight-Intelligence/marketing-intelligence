## Evidence it outperformed
Search results state the talk, given at Broome Community College in spring 2011, was posted to his blog and "quickly went viral" (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_Like_an_Artist and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Kleon). It became a 2012 Workman book and a New York Times bestseller per the Wikipedia summary. Another snippet in results says his books have sold over two million copies (not title-specific; not verified). A blog page-view figure was not found; sources conflicted, so: not verified.

## The move that transfers
The piece is built as a short list of numbered rules written as "what I wished someone told me," addressed to a younger self. That framing gives a scannable, quotable structure, and each item is small enough to be reshared alone. A talk prepared for a small live audience was published in full with its slides, so the blog post doubled as the product demo for a later book.

## What not to copy
The "steal" provocation and the ten-item count are Kleon's signature. Copying the title formula or his visual style would be obvious imitation.
